# Prior-art audit: RecipeQuest and structured skill-library learning

Audit date: 2026-10-07. Status: bounded audit of a project that has no results yet. Nothing below claims novelty; the purpose is to locate overlaps precisely enough that later claims can be scoped or dropped.

## 1. Scope and method

### Object of the audit

RecipeQuest is a small symbolic reinforcement-learning benchmark: a text-playable crafting quest whose state is a set of Boolean, persistent facts. Primitive operations gather a resource, activate a facility, attempt a recipe, wait, or submit the goal. A recipe is a directed hyperedge from a conjunction of prerequisite facts to one effect fact; some items have alternative recipes (OR across rules, AND within a rule). Reward is 1 only on terminal success. The audit was written when the following three staged mechanisms were specified; they are now implemented in this repository (Section 6 maps the audit's falsifying experiments onto the implemented controls):

- P1 (known structure): a set/attention baseline over tokenized entities with random per-entity identifiers, a role-typed bipartite fact-rule incidence encoder (equivalent to a two-stage hypergraph update), and a variant in which a sigmoid gate on each rule node, conditioned on recurrent memory, modulates its messages. All share one recurrent PPO actor-critic scoring variable-size candidate actions.
- P2 (unknown prerequisites): each rule declares a pool of 6 candidate base facts; the true prerequisite set is a hidden nonempty subset of size at most 3 (41 hypotheses). A log-space Bayesian updater with known failure noise epsilon uses (facts before, attempted rule, effect appeared) evidence. Versioned topology snapshots derive active incidences from the posterior credible set. A fixed-supergraph policy (all candidate edges plus posterior features) is compared with an adaptive-incidence policy (active edges plus the same features) and a recurrent evidence-only baseline.
- P3 (skills): candidate skills are mined from the agent's own trajectories (fragments ending in a newly achieved item fact, canonicalized by effect type), trained as target-conditioned closed-loop PPO controllers on a public subgoal reward, validated on separate instances, and admitted or rejected into an immutable versioned library with a call DAG. A manager is trained with duration-aware (semi-Markov) PPO. A 2x2 design forks one pretraining checkpoint into fixed or growing library, crossed with frozen or evidence-revised dependency structure. No language models are used inside the experiment.

Hypothesis to position: from public interaction evidence, an agent can learn and revise a compact structured library of closed-loop skills and conjunctive prerequisite relations, and this improves transfer to longer compositions beyond topology adaptation or skill discovery alone.

## 6. Mapping to the implemented controls

- (b) The `incidence` arm is the matched-information control proposed above: it concatenates the same recurrent memory into rule and fact updates and differs from `gated` only by the multiplicative gate. The `set` baseline gives every incidence token the identifiers of both endpoints (A1 style). Facts are never deleted, and the evaluator's exact reference solver (optimal plan length for monotone tasks) is reported next to every evaluation episode (item 10).
- (c) The fixed-supergraph arm receives the same posterior marginals and rule-level belief features as the adaptive arm, but not the credible-set mask as an explicit input feature. That stronger control is not implemented yet (docs/ROADMAP.md), so a positive adaptive-versus-fixed contrast could still be the representational convenience described in the table.
- (d) The interaction contrast is estimated by the summarizer when all four arms exist. The fixed-library control with extra training budget, the oracle-library control and the library-transplant diagnostic are not implemented.
- (e) No variant without declared pools exists yet; the information audit is in docs/METHODS.md (Section 2).

Overlaps. The walk itself is structure search over graphs (W1, by its common
description; the move set was not verified at metadata depth), and a version
space (W3) is the noise-free set of consistent nodes. W2 also places a
distribution over discrete structures and builds them by learned sequential
decisions, but samples a posterior over DAGs edge by edge rather than editing a
complete precondition hypothesis towards consistency. The `sample` walker is
posterior sampling in the sense of W5, whose regret guarantee is for tabular
MDPs and does not transfer automatically. The closest match to the learned
walk is W4: a policy trained with REINFORCE picks which variable to flip in
WalkSAT-style local search and, half of the time, falls back to a random
variable of an unsatisfied clause, which parallels the focused proposals; it
searches assignments of a known formula, whereas the walkers search hypotheses
about hidden preconditions inferred from logged evidence. W6 and W7 learn local

Decisive test. Overlaps. A PageRank-style proximity is not a new object: personalized PageRank with restart probability one minus the discount is the normalized successor representation of the same chain (M4, an elementary identity), and the successor representation (M1) is policy-dependent and adapts slowly when transitions change (M3), which is what evidence does in this testbed. One chain per context linked into a larger graph corresponds to memory networks (M17) and clone-structured graphs (M18); a factored transition model with learned conjunctive parent sets (N4, N5) is the hypergraph hypothesis in another notation.

No single theorem states that pairwise Markov proximity cannot represent conjunctive preconditions, but four facts cover the question. Random walks on hypergraphs with edge-independent vertex weights are equivalent to walks on a graph and do not use higher-order relations (M9); several hypergraph Laplacians are equivalent or nearly equivalent to those of the star or clique expansion (M10). The clique expansion is lossy: one recipe {a, b, c} -> g and the three recipes {a, b} -> g, {b, c} -> g, {a, c} -> g have the same unweighted clique expansion and different cheapest plans. The star expansion, one node per recipe, is lossless (M12), but a walk on it still sums over paths, an OR-like aggregation, whereas conjunctive reachability requires every tail of a hyperarc (B-connectivity, M11) and is evaluated by AND-OR propagation such as the additive and max heuristics (M13). A chain over complete fact sets represents conjunctions exactly but is the full state space. Finally, transition statistics learned from the agent's own data inherit its policy: under the brute-force node every pool member precedes every success, so pairwise statistics cannot separate required from unrequired candidates; only attempts with omissions can. Random walks appear in the checked work as exploration inside a planner (M14) or as retrieval scores, where learned path-typed walks outperform plain random walk with restart (M15), not as plan evaluators. The walker's Metropolis chain (Section 7) lives on hypotheses, not facts; personalized PageRank on that graph would be a discounted smoother of the same walk.

Decisive test. Offline, on evidence collected for the purpose (the earlier stages did not store episode logs), rank each recipe's candidates by posterior marginal, by personalized PageRank on the empirical fact graph and on the evidence-weighted fact-recipe graph, by an additive-heuristic score and at random, and score recovery of the hidden sets and the cost of the resulting plans; then evaluate pairs of worlds with equal clique expansions and pairs that differ only in hidden subsets observed under the brute-force policy. Theory predicts posterior first and the fact-graph score near chance on brute-force data. A 2x2 factorial (complete hypergraph node or pairwise graph, crossed with posterior sampling or proximity ranking) is warranted only if the proximity score carries information beyond the posterior, and its component selection must be confirmed on held-out worlds. Study M (docs/studies/M_markov_ranking.md) runs the offline step on selection worlds and the factorial on confirmation worlds; the factorial is run whatever the offline outcome, because testing the combinations is the question asked. Status: the reductions are `established`; a proximity-ordered proposal for the walk is at most a `potential empirical contribution`.

### 8.5 Keeping the four claims separate

I1 is the substrate: the other studies use its families, worlds as units and one pre-registered primary contrast each, with separate namespaces for selection and confirmation worlds. I2 is a claim about a ranking score given fixed evidence and is first run offline so that the sampling mechanism is held constant. I3 is a claim about discovery cost; I4 is a claim about how discoveries are represented and is first run with oracle discoveries. The outcomes of the four studies are summarized in docs/RESULTS.md, Section 4.5. A promoted composite is a macro or skill, the role of the P3 admission and validation gate and of the macro-control ablation (D16). Study L adds no admission gate because a TechTree discovery is exact (its firing reveals the sequence), so there is nothing to validate; with learned controllers the P3 gate would apply.
